How it works
Traditional monitoring reads output signals — softmax probabilities, loss, reward. These are lagging indicators: by the time they degrade, failure has already propagated through the entire network. DeepDrift reads the hidden-state trajectory instead, measuring the kinetic energy of representations as they evolve through the model.
A short calibration phase (80–100 normal-operation steps) sets a non-parametric IQR threshold. Thereafter, each step is O(1): one L2-norm, one comparison.
Three-phase pattern in Vision Transformers
Phase 1 — Elevation (layers 1–7)
OOD velocity exceeds in-distribution by 15–25%. The network works harder to process unfamiliar features. Per-layer AUROC peaks at 0.983 (layer 5).
Phase 2 — Compensation (layers 8–10)
Paradoxically, OOD velocity drops below in-distribution. Deep layers force anomalous representations toward the learned manifold — a form of "denial". AUROC inverts (<0.5).
Phase 3 — Terminal Spike (layer 11)
+26.8% OOD velocity at the final layer. The classification head cannot reconcile the compressed anomalous representation with its learned prototypes.
